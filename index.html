Project 1  ·  Characters and Story

The Tragedy of
Doctor Faustus

by Christopher Marlowe

A doctor of Wittenberg University gives his soul to Lucifer in return for twenty-four years of life and a devil as his servant.

Prepared and presented byNida Öcek
Good morning. My topic is The Tragedy of Doctor Faustus, in a simplified edition of the story. I will introduce the main characters, explain where the story happens, and tell the main events in order. It begins in Germany with an intelligent young man, and it ends in his study at midnight. The book is divided into nine parts, and I will follow them one by one.

Let me introduce the main characters. Doctor Faustus is an ambitious scholar who wants power. Mephistophilis is a devil, the servant of Lucifer, and he serves Faustus for twenty-four years. Lucifer was an angel once, but God threw him into hell. A good angel and a bad angel argue about his choices. Wagner is his servant, Valdes and Cornelius teach him magic, an old man asks him to repent, and two scholars are his old friends.

Finally, the spirit of Helen of Troy appears.

Setting

Where the story happens

Now, where does the story happen? Faustus is born in a town called Rhode, in Germany, and he studies at Wittenberg University. After the contract, he tours the universe for eight days. Then he travels through Germany, France and Italy, and arrives in Rome. Later he goes back to Wittenberg and visits the Emperor, meets a horse-dealer at an inn, and visits the Duke of Vanholt. The story ends in his study in Wittenberg.

In Parts One and Two Faustus is bored with his studies. Logic, medicine and law do not satisfy him. In the Bible he reads that everyone sins, so he thinks we all go to hell. He chooses magic. Valdes and Cornelius teach him, and he draws a magic circle. After thunder, a devil appears and returns as a friar. It is Mephistophilis, who came because he heard Faustus cursing God. Faustus offers his soul for twenty-four years.

In Part Three Faustus signs a contract in his own blood. First, the blood dries in the pen. Mephistophilis warms the pen over some coal. Faustus writes and signs, and he cries that it is done. Then the words Homo fuge appear on his arm, and richly dressed devils dance. Mephistophilis says hell is everywhere, but Faustus thinks it is a fable. When he asks for a wife, an ugly female devil appears. Then Mephistophilis gives him a book of spells.

In Part Four Faustus has doubts. He says he wants to repent, and the good and bad angels argue. He asks Mephistophilis who made the world, but the devil refuses to answer. Then Faustus prays to Christ. Lucifer, Beelzebub and Mephistophilis appear and say that he is breaking the agreement. To amuse him, they bring the Seven Deadly Sins: Pride, Covetousness, Envy, Wrath, Gluttony, Sloth and Lechery.

Next come the travels and tricks. Faustus and Mephistophilis tour the universe for eight days. In Rome, disguised as two Cardinals, they free Bruno. At the feast Faustus is invisible, snatches the Pope’s food and hits his ear. At the Emperor’s court he shows Alexander the Great and puts horns on Benvolio. He sells his horse to a horse-dealer, who finds a bundle of straw in the river. For the Duchess of Vanholt, he brings grapes in winter.

3 · The Old Man

Asks Faustus to repent. Mephistophilis hands him a dagger.

Mephistophilis threatens him. Faustus offers to write another letter in blood confirming the agreement.

5 · The lover

Helen returns as his lover: “Your kisses take my immortal soul.”

In Part Eight the twenty-four years are nearly over. Wagner knows that Faustus has made a will. At a dinner, Faustus shows Helen of Troy to two scholars. Later an old man asks him to repent, and Mephistophilis hands him a dagger. When Mephistophilis threatens him, Faustus offers to write another letter in blood, confirming the agreement. Then he asks for Helen as his lover and says that her kisses take his immortal soul.

In Part Nine the twenty-four years end. The two scholars pray in the next room. Mephistophilis tells Faustus that he turned the pages of the Bible on the night Faustus chose magic. The clock strikes eleven, and later the half-hour. At midnight there is thunder, and Lucifer and the devils enter. Faustus cries out for one more minute. A few hours later the scholars come into the study and find him torn to pieces by the devils.

To sum up, the story has four steps. First, Faustus chooses magic and offers his soul. Second, he signs a contract in blood, and Mephistophilis becomes his servant. Third, he spends twenty-four years on journeys, tricks and visits to important people. Finally, at midnight, Lucifer and the devils arrive, and the scholars find him torn to pieces.
